\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented GALASH, a pipeline that composes frozen DINO-family and SAM-family
foundation models into a parameter-efficient change detector. Two small
trainable modules — Cross-Change Attention and Bridge v2 — suffice to adapt
generic self-supervised features to bi-temporal change, achieving results
competitive with or surpassing recent specialist architectures on six standard
benchmarks while training only \textsc{TODO}\% of parameters.

\paragraph{Limitations.} GALASH inherits the resolution and patch-size
constraints of its backbones: DINO patch sizes of 14--16 mean that fine
sub-patch changes can be missed. The frozen-encoder design caps adaptation
capacity; some benchmarks (notably S2Looking) may still benefit from targeted
fine-tuning of the backbone. Finally, our SECOND results collapse the six
semantic classes to binary; extending the decoder head to multi-class
semantic CD is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Broader directions.} The encoder/decoder registry is a research
platform in its own right: studying how foundation-model pretraining
(general, remote-sensing, satellite) interacts with mask-decoder capacity
suggests a research agenda beyond change detection. We plan to extend GALASH
to dense multi-task remote-sensing (semantic CD, building footprint
extraction, disaster damage assessment) and to cross-domain transfer
(medical volumetric CD).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All code, SLURM launch scripts, dataset
preparation recipes, and trained checkpoints are released at
\url{https://github.com/anonymous/galash}. Training a single GALASH baseline
takes \textsc{TODO}~GPU-hours on one NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada.
