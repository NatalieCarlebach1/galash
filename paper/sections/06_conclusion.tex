\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented GALASH, a pipeline that composes frozen DINO-family and
SAM-family foundation models into a parameter-efficient change detector.
Two small trainable modules --- Cross-Change Attention and Bridge v2 ---
suffice to adapt generic self-supervised features to bi-temporal change.

\paragraph{Headline results.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item State-of-the-art on \textbf{DSIFN-CD} (96.74 F1; $+0.09$ over
    DDPM-CD), with $27\times$ fewer trainable parameters.
  \item State-of-the-art on \textbf{SECOND} binary change (73.20 F1;
    $+0.08$ over UniChange), without an MLLM.
  \item Within $0.87$ F1 of SChanger on \textbf{CDD} and $2.21$ F1 on
    \textbf{LEVIR-CD}, while using a single training pass and no
    synthetic-pair pretraining.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Ablation findings worth highlighting.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SAM decoder capacity is irrelevant for change detection.}
    SAM2.1-tiny ($39$ M) ties or beats SAM2.1-large ($225$ M) by up to
    $0.33$ F1 across all sizes (Table~\ref{tab:dec-sweep}). The bridge
    --- not the decoder --- is the locus of adaptation.
  \item \textbf{Remote-sensing pretraining beats raw model capacity.}
    DINOv2-RS-base ($86$ M) outperforms DINOv2-large ($304$ M) by $4.7$
    F1 on LEVIR-CD; pretraining domain dominates over scale.
  \item \textbf{Aerial pretraining (DINOv3-sat) helps only on
    aerial-distribution benchmarks.} It wins on S2Looking but loses on
    LEVIR-CD by $1.4$ F1 --- the SAT-493M corpus over-fits to coarse
    satellite imagery and loses LEVIR-CD's building-edge sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{Cheap inference-time wins (val threshold search +
    multi-resolution test) add $+0.66$ F1 on LEVIR-CD} with no
    retraining and no extra parameters.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limitations.} GALASH inherits the resolution and patch-size
constraints of its backbones: DINO patch sizes of 14--16 mean that fine
sub-patch changes can be missed. The frozen-encoder design caps adaptation
capacity; some benchmarks (notably S2Looking and LEVIR-CD+) likely require
synthetic-pair pretraining (e.g., Changen) to close the remaining $9$--$10$
F1 gap to SOTA. Finally, our SECOND results collapse the six semantic
classes to binary; extending the decoder head to multi-class semantic CD
is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Broader directions.} The encoder/decoder registry is a research
platform in its own right: studying how foundation-model pretraining
(general, remote-sensing, satellite) interacts with mask-decoder capacity
suggests a research agenda beyond change detection. We plan to extend
GALASH to dense multi-task remote-sensing (semantic CD, building footprint
extraction, disaster damage assessment) and to cross-domain transfer
(medical volumetric CD, where preliminary results on MSSEG-2 and ISBI-2015
are reported in the supplement).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} All code, SLURM launch scripts, dataset
preparation recipes, per-dataset YAML augmentation configs (matched to
each published SOTA pipeline), trained checkpoints, and the live
SOTA-gap monitor are released at
\url{https://github.com/anonymous/galash}. Training a single GALASH
baseline takes 2--6 GPU-hours on one NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada (48 GB).
