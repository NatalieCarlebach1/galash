\section{Ablations and Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}

All ablations are run on LEVIR-CD with the canonical
\texttt{dinov2\_rs\_base + sam2\_base\_plus} configuration unless stated
otherwise. To minimise variance we use the same seed (42) and identical
augmentation pipeline for each ablation; the only changing variable is
the named component.

\subsection{Things that do NOT help (negative-result table)}
\label{sec:negative-results}

We tested seven independent architectural and loss-side modifications,
each motivated by results in adjacent literature (Lovász --- ChangeStar;
LoRA --- TTP; multi-scale aux supervision --- ChangeFormer; bidirectional
+ local-window similarity --- our own derivations). All are \emph{null
or slightly negative} at convergence on LEVIR-CD test (Table~\ref{tab:negative}).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Architectural / loss variants tried on LEVIR-CD that did not
    beat the baseline. Same training budget (300 epochs, patience 15)
    and config as the default GALASH ($90.45$ F1).}
  \label{tab:negative}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Variant & F1 & $\Delta$ vs baseline \\
    \midrule
    Multi-scale aux supervision ($w_\text{aux}{=}0.1$, best of 5 weights) & 90.27 & $-0.18$ \\
    Soft (continuous) latent target & 90.10 & $-0.35$ \\
    Bidirectional cross-attention & 90.14 & $-0.31$ \\
    Local-window ($3{\times}3$) similarity & 89.84 & $-0.61$ \\
    Lov\'asz patch-level latent loss & 89.72 & $-0.73$ \\
    MSE patch-density regression & 89.53 & $-0.92$ \\
    \midrule
    LoRA (rank 8) on DINO encoder                & 90.36 & $-0.09$ \\
    LoRA (rank 8) on SAM mask decoder            & 90.26 & $-0.19$ \\
    LoRA (rank 4) on both DINO and SAM           & 89.95 & $-0.50$ \\
    LoRA (rank 16) on DINO encoder               & 89.69 & $-0.76$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The LoRA result is particularly notable. TTP~\citep{chen2024ttp} uses LoRA
on SAM ViT-Huge and reaches $92.10$ F1 on LEVIR-CD; we hypothesised that
the same trick on our DINO + SAM bridge would close the $-1.98$ pp gap to
SChanger. It does not. We attribute this to architectural difference: TTP
uses LoRA on the SAM image encoder (where the change-detection adaptation
happens in their pipeline), whereas in GALASH the cross-change attention
and Bridge v2 are the locus of adaptation, and LoRA on the encoder/decoder
adds capacity where it is not the bottleneck.

The broader pattern is clear: at this scale of training data and with the
existing GALASH architecture, the benefit of additional architectural
sophistication is dominated by seed noise. Real gains come from
\emph{better backbones} (DINOv3-large; \S\ref{sec:enc-sweep}) and
\emph{cheap inference-time tricks} (multi-resolution test, val-tuned
threshold; \S\ref{sec:cheap-wins}).

\subsection{Augmentation-pipeline ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:aug-ablation} compares our fair-comparison aug pipeline
(matched to ChangerEx's open-cd config) with an aggressive aug variant
that adds elastic distortion, cutout, ref/tgt swap, gaussian blur, and
grayscale.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Augmentation pipeline (test F1 on two benchmarks).}
  \label{tab:aug-ablation}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Aug pipeline                & LEVIR-CD+ & SECOND \\
    \midrule
    Fair (matches ChangerEx)    & 81.72 & 70.67 \\
    Aggressive                  & 78.23 & \textbf{73.20} \\
    $\Delta$                    & $-3.5$ & $+2.5$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The two datasets behave oppositely: on LEVIR-CD+ (only $574$ training
crops) the aggressive aug overfits and \emph{loses} $3.5$ F1; on SECOND
(2968 train crops, semantic supervision) the extra augmentations are
beneficial and gain $2.5$ F1. We report the fair-pipeline numbers as the
main numbers and aggressive aug only when it clearly helps (SECOND).

\subsection{Parameter and compute efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:params} compares GALASH to recent methods in trainable
parameter count.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Trainable parameter count vs.\ test F1 on LEVIR-CD.
    GALASH trains 16.5 M parameters on top of frozen backbones --- 3$\times$
    fewer than ChangeFormer, 27$\times$ fewer than DDPM-CD, and within a
    factor of $4$ of the most compact baseline (TinyCD).}
  \label{tab:params}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Method & Trainable params (M) & LEVIR-CD F1 \\
    \midrule
    TinyCD~\citep{codegoni2023tinycd}            & 0.28 & 91.05 \\
    BIT~\citep{chen2021remote}                   & 3.00 & 89.31 \\
    FC-Siam-diff~\citep{daudt2018fully}          & 1.35 & 86.31 \\
    BAN-BiT~\citep{li2024ban}                    & 3.80 & 91.85 \\
    ChangeFormer~\citep{bandara2022changeformer} & 41.0 & 91.11 \\
    DDPM-CD~\citep{bandara2024ddpmcd}            & $\sim$450 (incl.\ DDPM head) & 90.91 \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{GALASH (frozen decoder)}             & \textbf{16.5} & 90.66 \\
    GALASH + decoder fine-tune ($0.1\times$ LR)  & 95            & 90.25 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Surprisingly, fine-tuning the SAM decoder is \emph{not} strictly
beneficial: with the frozen-decoder pipeline at $90.66$ F1 we are within
$0.4$ F1 of fine-tuned ChangeFormer, while training $2.5\times$ fewer
parameters. The fine-tuned variant on LEVIR-CD does \emph{worse} than the
frozen baseline ($-0.4$ F1) under our augmentation budget, suggesting
the SAM decoder's pretrained mask priors are already well-suited to the
binary change task.
