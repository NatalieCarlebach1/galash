Bi-temporal change detection in aerial and satellite imagery has been dominated by
task-specific convolutional and transformer architectures, while the broader vision
community has converged on large self-supervised backbones such as
\textsc{DINOv2}, \textsc{DINOv3}, and the \textsc{SAM} family. We ask whether these
foundation models can be \emph{composed} --- rather than fine-tuned --- to obtain
competitive change-detection performance. We introduce \textbf{GALASH}, a
unified pipeline that pairs any frozen \textsc{DINO}-style encoder with any
\textsc{SAM}-family mask decoder via two lightweight trainable modules:
(i) a \emph{cross-change attention} operator that uses a learnable temperature to
produce per-patch change tokens from reference and target features, and (ii) a
multi-scale \emph{Bridge v2} that fuses DINO features into SAM's prompt and
image-embedding interfaces, repurposing the normally-unused dense-prompt slot as
a change-conditioning signal. Our encoder/decoder registry spans
\textbf{16 encoders $\times$ 8 decoders} (five \textsc{DINO} variants including
remote-sensing and satellite pretraining, three \textsc{SAM} generations), giving
a principled testbed for studying which foundation-model pairings transfer best
to change detection. On six standard benchmarks (LEVIR-CD, LEVIR-CD+,
S2Looking, CDD, DSIFN-CD, SECOND) GALASH trains only $\sim$\textbf{16.5 M parameters}
on top of frozen backbones and attains results competitive with or surpassing recent
dedicated methods. We release code, checkpoints, and all sweep logs.
